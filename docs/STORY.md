# The campaign: draft for approval

*Approved by the owner in round 171 (the open questions at the end take their planned answers: keep the twist,
Vane unaging, the names as written, the opening skippable on later characters). Round 171 built the prologue and
Region I's chapter; each later round builds the next part.*

## The short version

Vael's dead have been rising for forty-four years. The Choir says a Saint goes down
into the Wound each year to put them back. That is true, but it is not why the dead
rise, and it is not what the Saint is.

Four hundred years ago a woman came out of the sea carrying a lantern, during a plague
that was emptying the whole country. Her name was **Osanne**. She walked inland, and
wherever she passed, the dying stopped dying. In a cave under a small pilgrim town she
said she would carry death down with her, so the living would not have to. She went
down. The plague ended. The cave became the Wound, and the town became Vael.

Death did not go away. Osanne holds it at the bottom of the Wound, and she has been
holding it for four hundred years. Forty-four years ago she started to fail. Death
began to leak back up, and the dead began to rise. They are not attacking Vael. They
are what spills over.

Osanne cannot let go on her own. She needs someone to come all the way down and
release her. Every Risen is a failed try at raising that someone: a body without a
mind. **The Saint is the one that worked.** The Saint is Risen too: the only one who
came up whole, with a mind, and with her call in their head. That is why the Saint
cannot stay dead: every time the Saint falls in the Wound, it sends the Saint back
up in a new body. The old bodies stay where they fell.

The Choir found this out forty-four years ago and turned it into a business. If
Osanne is released, death comes back to Vael for good: the dead stay down, but so do
the Choir's elders, who have not aged a day since she went down (Archdeacon Vane
among them; he has led the Choir for four hundred years). So the Choir sends the
Saint down every year for the crowds and the wagers, and rings the bells at every
return so the Saint forgets the call.

The campaign is the Saint working all of this out, mostly outside the city walls,
and choosing to go down for real.

## How it plays

- **Vael** stays the hub. The **Pilgrims' Gate** (shut for forty-four years) opens after the
  first station and leads out into the world.
- **The world** is four outdoor regions, opened one at a time. Each takes about twenty
  minutes the first time: a camp to start from, roads and wild ground between points
  of interest, roaming packs, a few people with quests, lore to find and an optional
  world boss.
- **Story stations** end each region: a hand-made descent (the current chapter maps,
  moved and expanded) with a Warden. The Wound turns out to have more mouths than the
  one under the cathedral: it runs under the whole country, and it opens in old places
  (a barrow, a drowned church, a burnt abbey).
- **After a station** you go back to Vael (a Waystone at every camp), townsfolk have
  something new to say, and the next region opens.
- **Lore** comes from things you find (carvings, letters, bodies with notes, bells,
  graves), from people in the world and from townsfolk whose lines change after each
  station. Found pages collect in a new Codex section, **The Unwritten Gospel**, with
  a "% written" count (from the original design: *the world remembers what you forget*).
- **Quests:** each region has a main line (3-5 steps leading to its station), 3-4 side
  quests from people out there, and the daily and weekly quests keep working anywhere.
- **Regions stay open** after the story for farming. Their monsters scale to your best
  Station, like the cathedral's descents.
- **The cathedral's Stations I-XXX** stay as the endgame. After the campaign they are
  the road down to Osanne. The endings (from the original design) wait at the bottom
  for a later round.

## The prologue (much longer, before the first level)

1. **The bells (narrated, about one minute).** Dark screen cards over still pictures, a
   narrator's voice in text: Vael on its hill; the Plague of the Risen ("forty-four years
   ago the dead of Vael stopped staying dead"); the Choir and its cathedral over the Wound;
   the gates shut since that year; the yearly Descent and the city watching in the
   tavern's scrying pool; the bells. Last card: "This year, the Saint was found on the
   cathedral steps at dawn."
2. **The Vestry.** Odile wakes you (today's prologue, longer): what Vael is, what a Saint
   is in the Choir's telling, why you remember nothing ("it is the Wound's mercy, the
   Choir says"). She gives you the Saint's robe.
3. **A walk through Vael (playable, with a marker to follow).** Odile sends you on errands
   that each show a place and a slice of the city:
   - Hollis at the forge hands you your weapon and talks about the shut gates ("no iron has
     come in for forty years; I melt down the dead's buckles").
   - Brother Pell sells you nothing and tells you the Choir's story of Saint Osanne
     ("the Choir's own saint, painted in every chapel, though nobody remembers why she
     carries a lantern").
   - The Gilded Lamb: the Bookie shows the slate with the odds on you, and the scrying pool.
   - The Quiet Acre: the Crier and the graves, which are chained shut.
4. **Night (the first fight, in town).** The Acre's chains give way and the Risen climb out.
   You learn to move, dash and cast in the graveyard, with Odile calling out each thing.
5. **Vane at the cathedral doors.** The Descent, the Choir, the duty. He sends you down.
6. **The Waking** (station 0) as today, with the message in your hand.

About ten to fifteen minutes before the first descent starts.

## The regions

### Region I: the Hollow Moor

Fog, heather, standing stones, a gibbet road, burial mounds. The first ground outside the
walls in forty-four years.

- **Why you go:** a Choir tithe convoy went out to the moor villages a month ago and did not
  come back. Vane opens the gate and sends you after it (he wants the tithe, not the men).
- **Camp:** a shepherd's fold by the gate road, kept by **Wenna**, a moor shepherd who never
  left.
- **Main line:** follow the convoy's tracks; find the wagons at the gibbet crossroads (the
  guards were not killed by the Risen; they were killed with Choir crossbows); find the one
  survivor, **Sergeant Brask**, hiding in a barrow; learn the convoy carried something
  besides tithe: a sealed chest of ledgers bound for the Choir's archive in the old barrow
  vault; follow it down into **the Gibbet Barrow**.
- **Side quests:** Wenna's flock (lost sheep among the stones, and what is eating them);
  the barrow-wife who still leaves bread on the mounds; a moor child who collects finger-bells.
- **Lore:** the barrows are empty and were dug out from the inside; the old moor people
  buried their dead with a bell on one finger so they could ring if they woke; the stone
  circle's carving of a woman walking up from the sea with a lantern, and the dead lying
  down behind her.
- **World boss:** the Charnel King, in the largest barrow.

### Region II: the Sallow Fen

Flooded villages, reed beds, bell towers standing in the water, causeways.

- **Why you go:** salt water is rising in Vael's wells (today's chapter 3 opening, from Hollis).
- **Camp:** a punt-house on stilts, kept by **Old Tobe**, a punt-man.
- **Main line:** follow the salt to the old sluice; find the Choir's dam and its order to
  flood the low villages ("their dead rise too; let the water keep them down"); find
  **Sister Marl**, a nun who ran from the Choir forty years ago and lives in a drowned bell
  tower; she tells you she saw a child walk up out of the Wound forty-four years ago,
  whole, saying a woman was calling; open the sluice and go down into **the Drowned Way**.
- **Side quests:** Tobe's drowned village (ring its bell so its dead lie still); eel-men in
  the reeds; a smuggler who runs salt and news past the Choir.
- **Lore:** drowned houses with tables still laid; the Choir's flood order; the first Saint
  who walked out of the Wound; in the station, the drowned body with your scars.
- **World boss:** the Drowned Organist, playing in a sunken church.

### Region III: the Ashfields

Burnt farmland, ash fields, ruined mills, scarecrows, a plague-pit hamlet, a foundry.

- **Why you go:** Odile asks you to find the Wall of Names (today's chapter 4) and says the
  masons who cut it came from the Ashfields abbey, burned the year the dead first rose.
- **Camp:** a mill that still turns, kept by **Ide**, the miller's daughter.
- **Main line:** find out why the abbey burned (it hid the lantern faith, the older faith of
  Osanne); find the bell foundry where the Choir's bells were cast and learn the first bell
  was cast around Osanne's own lantern bell; find the abbey's portrait gallery, where every
  Archdeacon for four hundred years has Vane's face; go down under the abbey into **the
  Wall of Names**.
- **Side quests:** the scarecrows that move at night; a deserted tithe collector who wants
  to pay it back; Ide's brother, who went to Vael to be a Saint's squire and never wrote.
- **Lore:** burned tithe rolls; the lantern faith's hidden marks on doorframes; the
  foundry's casting book; the portraits.
- **World boss:** the Ash Widow, who walks the burnt rows.

### Back in Vael: Count the Bells

Today's chapter 5, longer. You know enough now to go into the belfry. You silence the
Bellwright and remember. Vane tells you the rest of the Choir's side: the loop, the forty-
four returns, why it must go on. Odile breaks with him. The guard at the coast road is
told to let you through.

### Region IV: the Saltreach

Salt cliffs, sea caves, wrecks, a lighthouse chapel on the rocks.

- **Why you go:** Odile tells you the lantern faith's last followers, **the Lanternfolk**,
  went to the coast when the Choir drove them out, and they would know what is at the bottom.
- **Camp:** a wreck turned into a shelter, kept by **Cass**, a wrecker.
- **Main line:** find the place Osanne came ashore (her footprints are cut into the rock and
  kept clear of salt by the Lanternfolk for four hundred years); find the Lanternfolk in the
  sea caves (they have waited for you: "she said one of hers would come walking from the
  city"); climb to the lighthouse chapel; go down into **the Lantern Chapel**, where the
  Choir's first inquisitor, **Our Lady of Nails**, still guards the Lanternfolk's book.
- **Lore:** **the Lantern Gospel**, Osanne's own words: why she went down, why death is
  leaking, what the Risen are and what the Saint is. Last line: "Send me someone who
  remembers."
- **After it:** Odile, Vane and the town react; the cathedral's Stations become the road
  down to Osanne.

### After the ending: the Abbey Isle (round 179)

Chapter 7, THE FLOODED UNDERCROFT, opens once the ending has played. A letter from Sister Hew says the water in the
abbey's undercroft has gone down and something is playing the old organ. On the isle: Sister Hew at the landing,
Brother Ansel by the fallen bells (the sisters let the sea in themselves to stop the Choir's men following Osanne),
and the sisters' rule on the cloister wall. Below, in the Drowned Nave, the Drowned Organist (he played for the
sisters before the Choir gave him the fen) and the abbess's unsent letters. Behind her desk a stair runs down under
the sea toward the Wound, and Osanne's lamp still burns at its foot: a hook for a later chapter.

## The people

- **Sister Odile:** woke you all forty-four times. Rang the bells too. Breaks with the Choir
  after the belfry.
- **Archdeacon Vane:** has led the Choir for four hundred years and does not age. Not a
  cartoon villain: he believes the loop keeps the city alive, and he is not wrong.
- **Osanne:** the woman from the sea; the Choir paints her in every chapel and has
  forgotten why she carries a lantern.
- **The Bellwright:** the Choir's first smith, as old as Vane.
- **Out in the world:** Wenna, Sergeant Brask, the barrow-wife (moor); Old Tobe, Sister Marl
  (fen); Ide, the deserter (Ashfields); Cass and the Lanternfolk (coast).
- **The Vesper and the Anchoress** play the same story; the lines that say crossbow or bolts
  go through the usual text swap.

## Lore pieces, about forty

Roughly ten a region, each a few short lines: carvings, letters, ledger pages, bodies with
notes, bells, graves, portraits, the Choir's orders. Each counts toward the Unwritten Gospel.
A few in each region are hidden off the roads.

## Older characters

Characters that finished today's story keep everything. The gate is open for them from the
start, the regions are there to explore, and the new story quests are offered from the
regions' camps, so they can play it.
